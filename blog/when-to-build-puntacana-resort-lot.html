When do you have to start building on your lot at Puntacana Resort & Club?

By ACCOM · Punta Cana, Dominican Republic · November 2026

The resort's own published buyer FAQ says the purchase agreement requires you to begin building your approved home within two years after closing, and that if there is a delay you request an extension specifying the reasons and the new dates proposed. That FAQ is dated July 2017, so treat it as what the agreement has historically required and confirm the term in your own agreement. We are ACCOM - construction and project management in Punta Cana, with 11 projects delivered since 2006 and Park View, a fully funded 60-apartment development, in construction at Puntacana West Village. This is general information, not legal advice.

When do I have to start building on my lot?

Per the resort's published FAQ: within two years after closing, for an approved home. The clock runs from closing, not from when you hire an architect, which is why owners who "wait for the right moment" often discover that the right moment was a year ago. The FAQ also says any architect or builder you choose must abide by the protective covenants and regulations that apply to all construction and landscaping in the community.

What happens if I do not build within the deadline?

The published FAQ describes the extension request as the route for a delay and does not state consequences, so we will not guess. What your agreement provides for a missed date is a question for the resort and your lawyer, and you should ask it before the date, not after.

Can I request an extension, and what does the request need?

According to the FAQ, yes. The request specifies the reasons for the delay and the new dates proposed. In practice, a request that arrives early, in writing, with a credible schedule is a stronger request than a late one. A credible schedule is one with an architect engaged and a design under way.

What has to be approved before construction can start?

Three layers, in order: the resort's approval of your design under the protective covenants, the permits required for the work (see our explainer on the new construction code and its transition to 10 April 2027), and a builder who can show they work within the community's rules. A builder who has already delivered inside the community, as we did with Tortuga Bay H2, knows the review process.

How long does approval plus construction actually take?

ACCOM commits to 16 months for the construction of a full custom villa. Before that comes design and approval time. The allowances below are ACCOM planning estimates, not resort rules, so confirm them with the resort.

What should a remote owner delegate?

Most owners with a lot at the resort do not live in Punta Cana. Delegate design coordination with the resort's review, permit filing, builder pricing against the budget, and supervision during construction to one accountable team, and keep the decisions that are yours: the design brief, the budget ceiling and the final sign-off.
