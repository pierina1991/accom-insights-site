The Dominican Republic's new construction code: what it means for your Punta Cana build

By ACCOM · Punta Cana, Dominican Republic · November 2026

The Dominican Republic has a new construction code in force since April 2026, and a one-year transition ends on 10 April 2027. Until then, a project filed for permitting can follow either the previous technical regime or the new code, and the choice has to be stated when the file is deposited. If your villa or development is in design right now, that is a dated decision with a wrong answer. We are ACCOM - construction and project management in Punta Cana, with 11 projects delivered since 2006 and Park View, a fully funded 60-apartment development, in construction at Puntacana West Village.

Is there a new construction code in the Dominican Republic?

Yes. The Ministry of Housing, Habitat and Buildings (MIVHED) issued Resolution 007-2026, dated 10 April 2026 and announced publicly at the end of that month. It officializes and puts into force the Dominican Republic Construction Code (CCRD) following approval by CONARTED. In the ministry's own words, the code sets the guidelines that govern the design, construction and supervision of works nationwide, and formalizing it by resolution was the step required for it to take effect.

Do I have to follow the new code right now?

Not yet in every case, which is why it matters. MIVHED set a one-year transition period running to 10 April 2027, and the rules during it are specific.

QuestionRule during the transition
Which regime applies?The applicant may elect the previous technical regime or the new code.
Where is the choice made?It must be stated expressly when the file is deposited at the Ventanilla Unica de Construccion.
Can I mix the two?No. Mixing criteria from both frameworks within one project is not admitted.
When does it end?10 April 2027.

Which regime should I choose for a project in design?

That is a decision for you, your architect and your structural engineer, and it should be made in writing before the file is deposited. Our view, from planning builds here: a project that has not been filed yet, or that is likely to still be in permitting when the transition ends, is better designed to the new code from the start, because switching regimes mid-process means redesign rather than a paperwork change. Confirm the rules with your designer and the ministry before relying on this.

What is a Supervisor Tecnico Privado, and how are private works inspected?

MIVHED habilitates and registers private technical supervisors (Supervisores Tecnicos Privados) under Decree 641-22 of 14 November 2022. According to the ministry, the register is open to companies as well as individual engineers and architects, the habilitation is valid five years, and MIVHED publishes a list of habilitated professionals. Under the ministry's inspection service, private works are inspected in three stages: initial, structural elements and final. In an optional modality, the first two are carried out by a habilitated private supervisor and the final inspection by the ministry. For an owner abroad, independent supervision is the practical way to know that what you pay for is what gets built.

How busy is permitting in Punta Cana?

MIVHED reported 201 construction licenses issued in July 2026, the highest monthly figure since the institution was created in 2021, and 41 of them were in Punta Cana, third after Santo Domingo (103) and Santiago (43). Between January and July it authorized 1,122 licenses, up 85% on the same period of 2025. More files in the system makes a clean, complete file more valuable.

What should an owner do now?

  • Ask your architect in writing which regime the project will be filed under, and why.
  • Make sure structural design and drawings follow one regime throughout.
  • Ask your builder how the choice affects schedule and price, in writing.
  • Plan independent supervision, with the three inspection stages in the schedule.
  • Watch the 10 April 2027 date if your permit is not yet filed.

The code interacts with cost as well as paperwork, which is why we keep cost per square meter and labor costs on the same page of every owner's planning.
