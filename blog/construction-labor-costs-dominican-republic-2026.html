Construction labor costs in the Dominican Republic jumped 12% in August. What does that mean for your villa budget?

By ACCOM · Punta Cana, Dominican Republic · October 2026

Construction costs in the Dominican Republic rose 6.42% over twelve months to August 2026, and labor alone jumped 12% in that single month. For a custom villa in Punta Cana, ACCOM estimates the full 20% wage increase adds roughly 6-8% to the build budget if it is passed through, and the second tranche has not landed yet. We are ACCOM - construction and project management in Punta Cana, with 11 projects delivered since 2006 and Park View, a fully funded 60-apartment development, in construction at Puntacana West Village.

How much did construction costs rise in the Dominican Republic in 2026?

The National Statistics Office (ONE) tracks the direct cost of building homes in its monthly ICDV index. Here is the latest reading we could verify at the time of writing, and we will update this page when the ONE publishes September.

Why did labor costs jump 12% in August?

The construction sector wage resolution (Resolucion CNS-04-2026) raised wages 12% from June 2026 and schedules another 8% from 1 June 2027, roughly 21% when the two compound. The index captures it with a lag, which is why the jump appeared in the August reading. Anyone who priced a build before June is working from an old labor number.

What does this do to the cost of a villa in Punta Cana?

Labor is roughly 30-40% of direct cost on a custom villa. On every US$1,000,000 of construction budget that is US$300,000-400,000 of labor, so the first 12% adds about US$36,000-48,000 and the 2027 tranche about US$26,000-35,000 more. Treat these as ACCOM planning estimates before any productivity effect; they are not official figures. This also helps explain why published per-square-meter ranges disagree so widely: many were set before June. For the wider picture, see what it really costs to build a villa in Punta Cana.

Is the increase finished, or is more coming?

More is coming. The 8% tranche on 1 June 2027 sits inside the design period plus the 16-month construction window of any villa that starts today. We commit to 16 months and plan every build against that schedule, which means the 2027 tranche belongs in your budget now, not as a surprise mid-build.

Does it affect a fixed-price contract I already signed?

It depends on the clause. In a true fixed-price contract the builder carries the wage risk. In a cost-plus or allowance-based contract you do. Ask for the escalation clause in writing and ask who pays for the June 2027 tranche.

What should I budget for a villa build right now?

Budget the full 20% labor increase and ask each bidder in writing how they price the second tranche. Tie payments to verified milestones, and put independent supervision on site so that what you pay for is what gets built. On the tax side, Law 30-26 (June 2026) applies a 10% final tax to individuals' real estate gains on a later sale, with the calculation base still pending a regulation, so confirm the details with your tax adviser. This is general information, not tax advice.

The difference between a budget that holds and one that drifts is local execution: current prices, supplier relationships and a team that reprices the plan when the numbers move. That is the job we do on our own projects, including Park View.
