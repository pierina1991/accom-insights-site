Law 30-26 and your Punta Cana villa: what to do now that the comment period has closed

By ACCOM · Punta Cana, Dominican Republic · Last updated 16 November 2026

Law 30-26 set a 10% single and final tax on individuals' real estate capital gains in the Dominican Republic, due within 6 months of the transfer, and it reduces the unified tax on real estate operations to 1% in 2027 and to zero in 2028. The regulation is still a draft: the public consultation closed on 11 November 2026, and the final regulation and the DGII general rule are pending. This page will be updated when they are issued. We are ACCOM - construction and project management in Punta Cana, with 11 projects delivered since 2006 and Park View, a fully funded 60-apartment development, in construction at Puntacana West Village. This is general information, not tax or legal advice; confirm every figure with your counsel.

How does Law 30-26 change the taxes on selling real estate in the Dominican Republic?

An individual who sells real estate now pays a 10% tax on the gain as a single, final payment under the new Article 296-1 of the Tax Code, within 6 months of the transfer. The unified tax on real estate operations falls from 2% to 1% in 2027 and is eliminated in 2028.

Is the Law 30-26 regulation final?

No. The law was promulgated on 18 June 2026; a 40-article draft regulation was posted by the Presidency's Legal Counsel on 7 September; the public consultation closed on 11 November 2026. The final text and the DGII general rule that will set the settlement procedure have not been published yet.

Does the primary-residence exemption cover a vacation villa in Punta Cana?

Under the draft, generally no. It excludes properties used mainly for vacation, rental or investment, unless the owner proves it is their only residential property and that they actually live in it.

How is the taxable gain calculated, and can I count my construction costs?

Neither the law nor the draft gives a method. Article 289 of the Tax Code, sale price minus inflation-adjusted acquisition cost, is the likely reference according to law-firm and trade-press analysis, but no published source yet says whether build and improvement costs count. For an owner who bought a lot and built a villa, that question decides most of the bill, so document the build as if it will count.

What records should I keep if I am building a villa now?

Keep one file your counsel can use, with land cost and build cost kept separate. ACCOM has delivered 11 projects since 2006, Serena 7 among them; this is the record file we recommend to every owner who builds:

  • The signed construction contract and the approved budget.
  • Proof of every milestone payment (bank transfers, not cash).
  • Supplier and subcontract invoices, with NCF where applicable.
  • Signed change orders, each with its cost.
  • Independent supervision reports, dated.
  • Permits, approvals and the final occupancy documents.
  • The lot purchase contract, the transfer tax receipt and the title.

Does buying or building off-plan count as reinvestment for the exemption?

According to the draft, no: pre-sale contracts, sales promises and options do not count. The reinvestment has to be a completed, registered purchase.
